# Policy and Law

> **Source:** `Module 3/3 Cyber Law  Ethics.pptx`, slides 2–12 · **Syllabus:** Unit III

## Key points

- Security staff must know the legal environment to **reduce liability and losses** from lawsuits.
- A **policy** acts like a law inside an organization, but **ignorance of a policy is an acceptable defense; ignorance of the law is not**.
- A policy is enforceable only if it meets **five criteria**: dissemination, review, comprehension, compliance, uniform enforcement.
- Laws come from four sources: constitutional, statutory, regulatory, common/case law.
- **Civil law** = disputes between parties (private); **criminal law** = harm to society (state prosecutes).
- Key laws: **CFAA, ECPA, HIPAA** (US), **Computer Misuse Act 1990** and others (UK), **US Copyright Law**.

---

## 1. Policy vs. law

Policies are rules an organization enforces on its staff. If someone is fired for breaking one, expect a legal challenge, so a policy must hold up in court. It does only if the organization can show all five:

| Criterion | The organization must show that… | Typical method |
|---|---|---|
| **Dissemination** (distribution) | the policy was readily available to the employee | hard copy, electronic copy |
| **Review** (reading) | it was given in a form the employee could read, including for illiterate, reading-impaired and non-English speakers | recordings in several languages |
| **Comprehension** (understanding) | the employee understood it | quizzes, assessments |
| **Compliance** (agreement) | the employee agreed to follow it | logon banner click, signed document |
| **Uniform enforcement** | it is applied equally, regardless of status or role | consistent discipline |

Only when **all five** hold can violators be penalized without legal risk.

## 2. Types of law

**By origin:**

| Type | Comes from |
|---|---|
| **Constitutional** | a constitution (national, state, local), bylaws or charter |
| **Statutory** | a legislature that writes and publishes statutes |
| **Regulatory / administrative** | the executive branch or a regulatory agency (executive orders, regulations) |
| **Common law / case law / precedent** | courts; interpretation based on earlier or higher-court decisions |

**By who is involved (within statutory law):**

| | Civil law | Criminal law |
|---|---|---|
| Concerns | relations between individuals and organizations (contract, employment, family, tort) | violations harmful to society (traffic, public order, property and personal damage) |
| Pursued by | the injured party, in civil court | the **state**, on the victim's behalf |
| Note | **Tort law**: redress for personal, physical or financial injury | |

## 3. General computer crime laws (US)

| Law | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Computer Fraud and Abuse Act (CFAA)** | Main federal computer-crime law. Part of the Comprehensive Crime Control Act 1984, amended 1996 with higher penalties. Punishment: **fines, up to 20 years in prison, or both**, depending on the value of the information and whether the offense was for **commercial advantage, private financial gain, or to further a criminal act**. |
| **Electronic Communications Privacy Act (ECPA) 1986** | The "wiretapping act". Regulates **interception of wire, electronic and oral communications**. |
| **HIPAA 1996** (Kennedy–Kassebaum Act) | Protects **confidentiality and security of healthcare data**. Requires security mechanisms, policies and procedures, plus a full assessment of systems handling health data. Sets rules for **electronic signatures** (integrity, user authentication, non-repudiation). |

HIPAA's five principles: **consumer control** of medical information; **boundaries** on its use; **accountability** for keeping it private; **balance** of public good against impact on the patient; **security** of health information.

## 4. US copyright law

Intellectual property is a protected asset, and copyright covers published works, **including electronic ones**.

**Fair use** allows copyrighted material for news reporting, education and scholarship if the use is **educational or library-related, non-commercial, and within reasonable limits**. Quoting excerpts for reference is fine when the **source is cited** and the work is **not passed off as original**.

## 5. UK computer security laws

| Law | Content |
|---|---|
| **Computer Misuse Act 1990** | Three offenses: (1) **unauthorized access** to computer material; (2) unauthorized access **with intent to commit further offenses**; (3) unauthorized acts **with intent (or recklessness) to impair** a computer's operation |
| **Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations 2003** | Replaced the 1999 regulations. Protects against **unwanted phone, e-mail and SMS** messages; sets rules for electronic marketing |
| **Police and Justice Act 2006** | Updated the Computer Misuse Act and its penalties; added an offense for **making or supplying tools** used in computer misuse |
| **Personal Internet Safety 2007** | A House of Lords committee report criticizing the government for **inaction on personal internet safety** (a report, not a statute) |
