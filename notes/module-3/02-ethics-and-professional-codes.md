# Ethics and Professional Codes

> **Source:** `Module 3/3 Cyber Law  Ethics.pptx`, slides 13–17 · **Syllabus:** Unit III

## Key points

- Ethical views on computer use **differ by culture**, and even within one country or company.
- **Education** is the main factor that evens out ethical views.
- Unethical or illegal acts come from **ignorance, accident or intent**; each needs a different deterrent.
- **Codes of ethics** from professional bodies deter lapses, especially where a **certification could be lost**.

---

## 1. Ethics and information security

Ethics is what is considered right or wrong behaviour, and it varies:

- **Across cultures.** Many Asian practices look like software piracy to Western cultures, because of Asian traditions of **collective ownership** versus the Western focus on **protecting intellectual property**.
- **Within a country, class or company.** Nationality is only one factor.
- Roughly **90% of software is created in the US**. The BSA's 2016 study found **39% of software installed worldwide in 2015 was not properly licensed**.

## 2. Ethics and education

**Education is the overriding factor** in leveling ethical perceptions in a group. Many employees lack technical training and may not realize their behaviour is unethical or illegal, so they must be trained on the expected conduct of an ethical employee.

## 3. Deterring unethical and illegal behavior

| Cause | Meaning | Deterrent |
|---|---|---|
| **Ignorance** | Not knowing the rules. *Ignorance of law is no excuse; ignorance of policy is.* | Design, publish and distribute policies; get everyone's agreement; run reminders, training and awareness programs |
| **Accident** | Harm done by authorized users with privileges (they are the likeliest to do it) | Careful planning and controls to prevent unintended changes |
| **Intent** | Criminal or unethical state of mind (intent usually must be proven to prosecute) | **Technical controls**, and vigorous prosecution if controls fail |

## 4. Codes of ethics of professional organizations

Professional bodies publish codes of conduct their members must follow. Codes improve judgment, and certified members may avoid lapses for fear of **losing their certification**.

- **Security professionals** must act ethically and follow their employer's policies, their professional body's code and the law.
- **Organizations** must develop, distribute and enforce their policies.

| Organization | Full name |
|---|---|
